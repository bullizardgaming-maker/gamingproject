This maps each requirement in the product spec to where and how it is implemented, and lists what is deliberately left for production hardening.

## 1. Escrow and payment orchestration

**Fiat (`apps/api/src/domain/orders.ts`, `ledger.ts`, `providers/payments/stripe.ts`)**
- Stripe Connect *separate charges and transfers*: the platform captures the payment through a Stripe-hosted Checkout Session; funds sit in the platform balance (escrow); on release a Transfer of the seller's net amount goes to their connected account. Commission never leaves the platform balance.
- Every money movement is a balanced posting in a **double-entry ledger** (`ledger_entries`, append-only via trigger). Accounts: `gateway_clearing`, `escrow`, `treasury`, `reserve`, `seller_available`, `payouts`, `platform_withdrawals`, `user_credit`, `fx_pnl`, `tax_payable`, `fx_position`, `fx_conversion`. Every posting balances within each currency. Balances are derived, never stored.
- State machine: `awaiting_payment → paid → delivered → completed`, with `disputed`, `refunded`, `cancelled`, `charged_back`. Every transition is guarded by the expected current status and recorded in `order_events` (append-only).
- Windows: `inspection_72h` for standard categories, `insurance_14d` for `risk_class = 'high'` (accounts). The worker releases escrow when the window lapses without a dispute and the order is not frozen.
- Split: commission is computed at order time from the seller's tier and locked on the order; 10% of each commission funds the chargeback reserve.
- Unpaid orders expire after `ORDER_PAYMENT_TTL_MINUTES` and return reserved stock. A payment that arrives after expiry is recorded and refunded.

**Web3 (`contracts/contracts/MarketEscrow.sol`, `apps/api/src/domain/web3.ts`, `indexer.ts`)**
- The API issues **EIP-712 terms signed by the platform** (seller, amount, fee, release schedule, deadlines). The contract only accepts deposits carrying that signature, so buyers cannot change the fee or recipient.
- Milestones: accounts release 30% on credential delivery and 70% after 14 days; boosting releases in four stages; everything else releases after 72h. Buyer can approve early; buyer can reclaim if nothing is delivered by the deadline.
- Disputes: either party freezes unreleased funds. A panel of 5 **bonded validators** is drawn from a future block hash, votes with **commit-reveal**, and the contract executes the majority (tie → 50/50 split). Majority voters share a 2% arbitration fee; validators who don't reveal lose 10% of their bond. Bonds are locked while assigned. No admin function can move escrowed funds.
- The indexer mirrors `EscrowCreated / DisputeOpened / EscrowSettled` into orders after 3 confirmations (auto-delivery runs off-chain on `EscrowCreated`).

**Money on an order.** `amount_cents` is the full order value in the settlement currency and is exactly what escrow holds. It is funded from up to three sources: a promo discount (`discount_cents`, paid by treasury), store credit (`credit_cents`, from the buyer's `user_credit` account) and the card (`charged_cents`, the rest). A database CHECK keeps the parts summing to the whole. Orders fully covered by credit/promo skip the PSP. Refunds send each part back where it came from.

**Currencies** (`domain/fx.ts`): everything internal (escrow, ledger, balances, commission) is in `SETTLEMENT_CURRENCY` (USD). Buyers can see and pay in EUR or GBP; sellers can price in any of them (a trigger keeps a settlement-currency price for search and sorting). At order time the rate, including an `FX_SPREAD_BPS` spread, is locked on the order with the presentment amount, so the buyer pays exactly what they were quoted. Differences between what the PSP settles and what was expected go to `fx_pnl`. Rates come from the ECB daily feed (`FX_PROVIDER=ecb`) or static config; stale rates (> `FX_MAX_AGE_HOURS`) stop foreign-currency checkout rather than guess.

**Promo codes & referrals** (`domain/promos.ts`, `referrals.ts`): admin-created codes (percent with an optional cap, or fixed amount; minimum order, first-order-only, per-user and total limits, validity window, optional category scope). A code is reserved at order creation under a row lock on the code, so a limited code can't be over-redeemed by concurrent checkouts; it is redeemed on capture and released on cancel/refund. The discount is platform-funded: sellers are paid on the full price. Referrals, G2G-style: on every completed order of a referred user the referrer earns `referral_settings.rate_bps` (20% by default, staff-set) of Bullizard's commission, paid from treasury to their withdrawable balance, while the referee stays active (completed orders no further apart than `active_days`); never between accounts sharing a device, card or wallet.

**Tax on commission & invoices** (`domain/tax.ts`, `invoices.ts`): commission is VAT-inclusive, so sellers' payouts don't change; when escrow releases, the tax inside the commission is fixed on the order and booked to `tax_payable` rather than revenue. The commission is a service to the seller, so the seller decides the treatment: EU consumer sellers pay their own country's rate (OSS), verified EU businesses elsewhere are reverse-charged (VAT numbers checked against VIES with `VAT_VALIDATION=vies`), sellers in the platform's country pay its rate, consumer sellers in non-EU countries listed in `TAX_REGISTERED_COUNTRIES` pay the local rate, and everyone else is outside scope. Rates live in `tax_rates` (EU standard rates plus GB/NO/CH/AU/NZ seeded; staff can schedule changes). Each month every seller gets an immutable commission invoice with gapless numbering per series and year, per-rate totals, the legal reverse-charge wording and a PDF. `GET /v1/admin/tax/report` (JSON or CSV) totals commission and tax by country, treatment and rate for returns. Collected tax leaves the PSP balance only through the four-eyes withdrawal flow (`source: tax_payable`). *Rates and treatments must be confirmed by a tax adviser before launch.*

**Seller currencies** (`domain/sellerfx.ts`): sellers pick USD, EUR or GBP for their balance and payouts. The mid-market rate is locked on the order when it's placed, and at release the seller is credited in their currency, so the buyer's conversion is the only one: a EUR 92 listing pays EUR 92 minus commission however the rate moves before release. The platform carries the currency position in per-currency `fx_position` accounts (chargeback debits and reversals go through the same rate, so a won chargeback restores the exact amount). Treasury converts money at the PSP or bank before paying out a currency and records it (`POST /v1/admin/fx/conversions`); `GET /v1/admin/fx/position` shows each currency's position, seller balances, expected PSP balance and shortfall to cover. Reconciliation checks each currency's PSP balance including conversions.

**Payout holds** (`domain/payoutholds.ts`): released money can be withdrawn after a hold that depends on track record: 7 days for new sellers, 3 once established (10+ sales, 30+ days, <1% chargebacks), none for trusted sellers (100+ sales, 180+ days, <0.5%), and 14 days after a recent chargeback at ≥1%. Staff can override per seller (audited). The dashboard shows what's withdrawable now, what's held, and when each amount unlocks.

**Refunds after release** (`domain/clawback.ts`): staff with `orders.refund_after_release` (arbiters, finance) can refund a completed order in full or in part, e.g. when an account is reclaimed by its original owner after the window. Every part is split pro rata. The card part goes back through the PSP, each refund with its own idempotency key; promo goes to treasury and store credit to the buyer. The commission, with the tax and reserve share inside it, is returned. The seller's share is clawed back from their balance (it may go negative, which is a debt repaid by their next sales and blocks withdrawals meanwhile), or covered by the platform or the reserve. Each refund issues an immutable credit note (gapless `BZ-CN-YYYY-NNNNNN`, linked to the invoice it corrects), notifies the seller and sends an `order.clawback` webhook. The tax report nets credit notes out, and a later chargeback is capped to what is still on the card.

**Seller accounting exports** (`domain/exports.ts`): sales, balance statement (opening, running and closing balances), payouts, invoices and credit notes, as CSV or JSON for any range up to a year, from the dashboard or the merchant API (`reports:read`). Amounts are integer cents with a currency column. CSV cells are escaped and spreadsheet formulas neutralised. Exports read from the replica.

**Manual ledger corrections** (`domain/adjustments.ts`): an admin proposes balanced lines with a reason (optionally tied to an order); a different admin approves, which posts them as one append-only transaction. Both steps are in the audit log; `GET /v1/admin/users/:id/ledger` shows a user's accounts for investigation.

**Treasury withdrawals** (`domain/treasury.ts`): platform earnings leave through a four-eyes flow: one admin requests, a different admin approves (enforced by a DB CHECK), then a PSP payout is made and booked to `platform_withdrawals`. The amount is limited to treasury minus the chargeback reserve. With withdrawals modelled, reconciliation checks the PSP balance for real.

## 2. Marketplace mechanics

- **Commission as a gross-up** (`domain/commission.ts`, migration 055): the seller sets their price and receives it exactly; the buyer pays `ceil(price ÷ (1 − rate))`. The rate is the seller's rank (`seller_ranks`: Hatchling 13.99% → Scale → Fang → Basilisk → Dragon 9.99% by trailing-30-day completed sales; names, thresholds and rates are staff-editable with `ranks.manage`) or the launch promo (5% for 60 days from the first listing or proposal, `commission_settings`), whichever is lower. `users.commission_rate_bps` is refreshed when an order is priced and hourly (`seller_rates` job); `GROSS_SQL` prices listings in SQL so public `price_cents`/`price_tiers` are buyer prices and `seller_price_cents`/`seller_price_tiers` the seller's. The rate and split are stored on each order. `GET /v1/seller/price-preview` gives the sell form the buyer price in every supported currency.
- **Launch phases and Founding Sellers** (migrations 058–059): `categories.phase` with `launch_settings.phase` (1: currency, items, top-ups; 2: + accounts, boosting; 3: + keys, gift cards, video games). `category_open(slug)` hides closed categories from search, the directory and listing pages and refuses listings in them. A seller whose first listing lands before `launch_settings.founding_until` is a Founding Seller for good (badge on listings and the shop page) and, while the window is open, may feature `founding_featured_slots` listings at a time for `featured_days` (`offers.featured_until`); featured listings lead every search ordering.
- **Launch games and in-game delivery** (`domain/attributeSets.ts`, `domain/leagues.ts`, migration 056): Aion / Aion 2 (server + region), Albion Online (server), Path of Exile 1 & 2 (league + Softcore/Hardcore). Challenge leagues live in `game_leagues` (support opens and ends them; the league field's options read the open ones live). When a league ends, listings in it from sellers not seen for 14 days expire (`offers.status = 'expired'`, `league_expiry` job, notification); active sellers keep theirs and anyone can relist into an open league. Games that deliver in-game (`handoverFieldFor`) require the buyer's character name or UID on the order (`orders.character_name`, carts too); it is remembered per game on the account (`users.handover_names`) so the next checkout is one tap. **Proof of delivery** (`DELIVERY_PROOF_REQUIRED`): a manual delivery of currency, items, top-ups or boosting needs a photo or video the seller uploaded to that order's chat (`proofAttachmentId`; MP4/WebM up to 50 MB are accepted, scanned and stored as sent; images are re-encoded as before); it is stored on the order (`delivery_proof_id`) and shown to the buyer.
- **Seller onboarding** (`domain/onboarding.ts`, migration 057): selling needs Tier 1 (email + phone) and a passed rules test (`rules_questions`, pass mark and questions staff-editable with `rules.manage`; attempts are counted, misses are explained). Identity (Tier 2) is due `id_due_days` (30) after the first listing: the `id_deadlines` job reminds at 7 and 1 days, then sets `selling_restricted_at`, pauses the seller's listings (`expired_reason = 'id_overdue'`) and blocks new listings; withdrawals already need Tier 2. KYC approval lifts the restriction and reactivates the listings.
- **Order refs, official order messages and the safety panel** (migration 061): every order carries `ref` (`order#` + 7 unambiguous characters) for people; UUIDs stay in URLs and APIs. The chat kind `system_order` is server-only (`postOrderCard`): a card with the ref, item, quantity, character name and a status chip, posted at creation and on paid / delivered / completed / disputed / refunded; the web renders it in a frame user messages never get. `site_texts.chat_safety` is the staff-managed "Stay safe" text shown above every order chat.
- **Game voting and creator codes** (`domain/gameVotes.ts`, `domain/affiliates.ts`, migration 060): anyone signed in suggests a game (three a day, de-duplicated by name, refused if already on the market) and votes once per suggestion; staff mark planned / added / declined. Creator affiliate codes are given by finance with a share of the commission; a buyer attaches one code for good (signup `creatorCode` or `POST /v1/me/affiliate`), and each completed fiat order pays the creator `floor(commission × share)` from the treasury into their seller balance (`affiliate_earnings`, idempotent per order), independent of the flat referral reward.
- **Auto-delivery** (`domain/inventory.ts`): bulk `.txt` / JSON upload (≤10,000 items per upload), each item AES-256-GCM encrypted with the offer id as AAD, de-duplicated by a keyed HMAC fingerprint. On payment capture items are delivered in the same transaction and both parties are notified over WebSocket.
- **Holiday mode** (`PUT /v1/seller/shop-status`): pausing hides the shop's listings from search and blocks new orders (optionally until a date, with a message on the listings); orders already paid still have to be delivered.
- **Delivery deadlines**: manual listings promise a delivery time (`delivery_hours`); the deadline is set when the order is paid. Past it, both parties are emailed, the buyer can cancel for a full refund (`POST /v1/orders/:id/cancel-late`), and after `LATE_AUTO_REFUND_HOURS` more the worker refunds automatically. Seller profiles show their on-time delivery rate.
- **Game-specific listing fields** (`domain/attributeSets.ts`, migration 028; launch games above): listings carry fields such as server, region, platform and rank, defined per game and category (e.g. League of Legends boosting: server, current and target rank; WoW gold: region, realm, faction; CS2 skins: exterior, StatTrak), with a default set per category for every other game. Values are validated on create and update (select values are matched without case and stored canonically). Search filters with `attr.<key>=<value>` and, for one game, returns per-value counts where each field's counts ignore its own filter, so the game page's pickers show what every option would give. The module has no imports, so the web app bundles the same definitions for the sell form and filters.
- **Watchlists and price alerts** (`domain/watches.ts`, migration 029): buyers watch a listing, or a slice of a game's market (game, optional category and fields), with an optional target in their display currency. The `price_alerts` worker job (every `WATCH_CHECK_MINUTES`, advisory lock) notifies once per new low at or below the target, or below the starting price when there is no target, and when a listing is back in stock. Notifications use the `alerts` category.
- **Seller analytics and business dashboard** (`domain/analytics.ts`, migration 030): listing views are counted once per viewer, listing and hour, never the seller's own. With `REDIS_URL` the de-duplication is a `SET NX` with a one-hour TTL (`lib/viewDedupe.ts`), so the count is exact across API nodes; without Redis, or while it is unreachable, each node de-duplicates in a bounded in-memory window. Sellers see sales, net, views, conversion, average order, repeat buyers, refund and dispute rates for 7/30/90/365 days against the previous period, with daily (weekly for a year) series and top listings. Staff with `analytics.view` (finance, compliance, admin) see GMV, take and take rate, dispute, card-chargeback and refund rates, new buyers and sellers, and breakdowns by category and game. A sale counts when paid; money is in the settlement currency.
- **Help centre and support tickets** (`domain/support.ts`, migration 031): articles ship with the web app (`src/lib/help.ts`). Customers open requests, optionally about one of their orders (buyer or seller side); order and payment problems on an order still in escrow are high priority. Staff with `support.manage` work a queue, reply, add internal notes the customer never sees, assign and resolve (all audited). At most five open requests per customer; requests waiting on the customer for a week resolve themselves.
- **Buyer requests** (`domain/requests.ts`, migration 033): a buyer posts what they want (game, category, any game fields they care about, optional budget); sellers who list in that game and category are notified (up to 50). Verified sellers send one proposal each (price, delivery time, message) and can revise or withdraw it until the buyer chooses. Accepting creates a manual-delivery listing with stock 1 **reserved for that buyer** (`offers.reserved_for`): it is hidden from search, game pages, shops and other users (404), only that buyer can order it, and its terms can't be edited. The buyer then pays through the normal checkout, so escrow, delivery deadlines and disputes apply unchanged. Requests expire after 14 days (worker); a buyer has at most 10 open. Requests and proposals refuse contact details.
- **Quantity prices** (`domain/priceTiers.ts`, migration 034): up to five tiers per listing (`minQty` from 2 upward, unit price strictly falling); an order pays the best tier it reaches, never above the base price, and the order stores that unit price. Accounts can't have tiers. The module has no imports, so the web app shows exactly the price the API charges.
- **Presence** (`domain/presence.ts`, migration 036): signed-in activity (API, merchant API, socket frames) updates `last_seen_at` at most once a minute per user; "online" is the last 5 minutes and sellers can turn it off. Response time is the median wait between a buyer message (one that follows a seller message or opens the chat) and the seller's next reply over 90 days, with silences over a day counted as a day; the worker recomputes it hourly and it shows once there are three answered questions.
- **Cart** (`domain/cart.ts`, migration 037): up to 20 listings, from any sellers, in one checkout. Each line becomes its own order (own escrow, chat, delivery and dispute) created by the normal order path, all tied to one `checkouts` row; if any line fails (stock, limits), the ones already created are cancelled. The buyer pays once: one PSP session for the total. On capture the amount is split across the orders in proportion (`splitProportional`, so cents add up exactly); a chargeback on the checkout opens one chargeback part per order (`<ref>:<orderId>`) and closing it closes them all. Refunds are per order.
- **Seller sales and discount codes** (`domain/discounts.ts`, migration 038): a timed sale (up to 30 days, below the list price) or a shop code (1–90% off, optional minimum order, total and per-buyer limits, start and end). Both are seller-funded, unlike platform promo codes: they lower the order's own price, so commission is on what the buyer pays. Price order: sale price, then quantity tier, then the seller code. Search, sorting and the settlement-price trigger use the effective price; listings carry `regular_price_cents` and `sale_ends_at` while a sale runs, and a worker job flips sales on and off.
- **Bundles** (migration 039): one listing made of 2–10 of the seller's own instant listings, at one price. A bundle has no inventory: buying it reserves each part's items against the order, and its stock is the number of complete sets (0 while any part is paused). `offer_available()` is the one SQL definition of stock for manual, instant and bundle listings.
- **Seller trust levels** (`domain/trust.ts`, migration 040): new → rising → trusted → top, from completed orders, account age, on-time delivery, dispute rate and chargeback rate (rising: 10 orders, 30 days, 85% on time, ≤5% disputes, ≤1% chargebacks; trusted: 100 / 180 days / 92% / 2% / 0.5%; top: 1,000 / 365 days / 97% / 1% / 0.3%). An hourly job caches the level for badges and the `trust` sort; `GET /v1/seller/trust` shows a seller what the next level needs.
- **Offering a listing on a request** (migration 041): a seller can answer a buyer request with one of their live listings that already matches it, instead of a custom price. Accepting it sends the buyer to that listing; no private copy is made.
- **Dispute case file** (`domain/disputes.ts`, migration 042): the buyer picks a category when opening a dispute, and each side can add up to five written statements. Arbiters get one case file (`GET /v1/admin/disputes/:id/case`, audited): timeline of order events, chat and statements, key facts (paid, delivered, confirmed, deadlines), the listing snapshot, payment and risk signals, and whether the chat hash chain verifies.
- **Saved searches** (`domain/savedSearches.ts`, migration 043): buyers save market or game-page filters (up to 20) and get an hourly or daily digest of new listings that match, the three cheapest shown, never their own listings.
- **Price history** (`domain/priceHistory.ts`, migration 045): a trigger records every change to a listing's buyable price (sales starting and ending included); an hourly job keeps the lowest and median live price per game, category and day (settlement currency). The web chart (`components/PriceChart.tsx`) draws a listing as a step line and a market as two lines, with a crosshair readout and a table view; its two colours were checked for colour-blind separation in both themes.
- **Buyers also bought** (`domain/recommendations.ts`): live listings bought by buyers of this one, counted only where at least two different buyers did (so no single buyer's purchases show), ranked by how many did; the order page tops it up with popular listings in the same game.
- **Account warranty** (`domain/warranty.ts`, migration 046): a seller can give an account listing a 7–90 day warranty that starts when escrow releases (card orders). That order's payout stays on hold until the warranty ends or an open claim is settled. The buyer claims (recovered, banned, locked); the seller has 48 hours to report a fix (the buyer confirms within 72 hours or escalates), refund, or contest; silence escalates or resolves automatically. Arbiters refund through the refund-after-release path (seller pays by default) or reject; every step is a system message in the order chat and decisions are audited.
- **Seller storefronts** (`domain/storefront.ts`, migration 047): a banner (byte-sniffed, malware-scanned, cropped to 1500×400 WebP with metadata stripped, stored encrypted and covered by key rotation, served with a sandbox CSP at a hash-versioned URL), an intro screened like a listing, an accent from a fixed palette and up to four pinned listings. Staff can clear a banner or intro (audited); account deletion removes them.
- **Bulk listings** (`domain/bulkListings.ts`): CSV export of every listing and a previewed import (up to 500 rows). Rows with an id update that listing; rows without create one; every row gets its own result, so one bad row never blocks the rest. The game, category, currency and delivery type of an existing listing can't change, and text is screened as usual.
- **Linked game accounts** (`domain/linkedAccounts.ts`, `providers/platformAccounts.ts`, migration 048): a seller signs in to Steam (OpenID 2.0; the assertion's return address must be ours and Steam itself must confirm it with `check_authentication`) or Riot (Riot Sign On, needs an approved app). The sign-in state is a single-use nonce valid for 10 minutes. An account listing for that platform's games can show the linked account: buyers see "Steam account verified · since 2014" (the creation date needs `STEAM_WEB_API_KEY`), never the account name, and staff see the name and profile next to the Original Owner Proof. One Bullizard user per platform account at a time: a second user trying to link it is refused and gets a risk flag for the fraud team. When the order completes, a trigger unlinks it from the seller and detaches it from listings, so the buyer can link it.
- **Boosting progress** (`domain/progress.ts`, migration 049): while a boosting order is paid and undelivered, the seller posts milestones (percent, never lower than the last; a note; optionally a screenshot already uploaded to the order chat, so it went through the same scan, re-encode and hash chain). Each one is a system message in the chat and a notification to the buyer in their language; the order page shows a progress bar and timeline.
- **Buyer reputation** (`domain/buyerReputation.ts`, migration 050): sellers rate the buyer once per completed order within 30 days, positive or negative, with tags from a fixed list that must fit the verdict (no free text, so nothing to moderate). The summary sellers see on orders (and buyers see on their account) also counts completed purchases, disputes the buyer opened and lost, and chargebacks from the order records, so a seller can't rate away a dispute the buyer won. "Take care" shows for any chargeback, two lost disputes, or more than a quarter negative from three or more ratings.
- **Retention dashboard** (`domain/cohorts.ts`): from paid orders by UTC month: buyer and seller cohorts by first purchase or sale (a heatmap of the share active each later month), the share of each cohort that ordered again within 90 days (cohorts younger than 90 days are marked and left out of the headline rate), returning buyers per month, and seller churn (sold last month, nothing this month). The running month shows counts so far and no churn.
- **Original Owner Proof + Security Changeover** (`domain/offers.ts`, `orders.ts`): sellers submit registration email/date/billing name; approval requires the billing name to match the eKYC legal name. Account deliveries create a six-step checklist that must be completed before the buyer can release early. Unverified account listings are shown as *High risk · secondary market* and lose Chargeback Shield coverage.

## 3. Chat and real-time

- `chat_messages` has a **server-assigned, gapless, monotonic `seq`** per room: the append runs `UPDATE chat_rooms SET last_seq = last_seq + 1 … RETURNING`, whose row lock serializes writers across all nodes.
- Each message stores `hash = sha256(prev_hash | seq | sender | kind | body | created_at)`. The table is append-only (trigger), and `GET /v1/chat/rooms/:id/verify` recomputes the chain, so even a DBA edit is detectable. This log is embedded in arbitration and in chargeback packets.
- **Per-user chat keys** (`domain/chatkeys.ts`, crypto-shredding): every user who writes in chat gets a random 32-byte key, stored wrapped by the data keyring (so key rotation re-wraps it). Their messages are sealed with it (AES-256-GCM, bound to the room and sequence number), and the hash chain (v2) covers HMAC(key, text) instead of the text. System messages stay readable and chain over SHA-256 of their text. Destroying a key erases everything that user wrote for everyone, while every chain still verifies over the stored digests; `/verify` reports how many messages are erased, and with the keys it also checks each readable message against its digest, so a swapped ciphertext is caught. Erasure requests: `POST /v1/admin/users/:id/erase-chat` (`retention.manage`, audited), refused while the user has an open dispute, chargeback or compliance case (the messages are evidence). Deleted accounts' keys are destroyed by the retention job once `RETENTION_ORDER_IP_DAYS` (the chargeback window) has passed. Messages written before this change keep the v1 format (text in the chain) and are removed with their room by the retention job.
- **Off-platform detection** (`domain/safety.ts`, shared with the web app) flags messenger names (Discord, Telegram, WhatsApp, Facebook/Messenger, Instagram, Snapchat…, including Arabic spellings and disguised ones: `squash()` undoes digit swaps and spacing, so "d1scord" and "d i s c o r d" match), outside payment methods ("friends and family", PayPal, Zelle, Vodafone Cash, InstaPay…), "pay outside the site", phone numbers, email addresses (allowed only in account-order rooms, where the handover needs one), links to anything but bullizard.*, @handles and "add me" phrasings. The chat warns before sending; on send the server posts the launch wording ("Sharing outside contacts can get your account banned right away.", never a strike count), counts a strike (ban at `OFFPLATFORM_SUSPEND_STRIKES`, default 3; 1 = instant ban) and puts the message in the staff queue with two messages of context. Moderators confirm it or dismiss a false alarm, which takes the strike back.
- **Listing moderation** (`domain/moderation.ts`, migration 035): a listing's title and description are checked against a staff-maintained term list on create and edit. `block` terms (clearly illegal goods) refuse the listing; `review` terms, and contact details, hold it with status `review` (hidden from search and from everyone but its seller and staff) until a moderator approves or rejects it with a reason; the seller is notified either way and can't switch it live themselves. Matching ignores case, digit-for-letter swaps and separators. Staff with `moderation.manage` (arbiters, support, admin) work both queues and edit the terms; every decision and every read of chat excerpts is audited.
- **Screenshot evidence** (`domain/attachments.ts`, `providers/files.ts`): buyers and sellers can attach PNG/JPEG/WebP images to an order chat (8 MB, 20 per hour). The real type is sniffed from the bytes, the file is scanned with ClamAV (`MALWARE_SCANNER=clamd`, required in production; an infected upload is refused and flags the uploader), then decoded and re-encoded with sharp, which strips EXIF/GPS metadata and anything hidden after the image data. Files are stored encrypted on disk or in S3; the chat message records the SHA-256, so the hash chain proves evidence wasn't swapped later. Attachments are served with `nosniff` and a sandbox CSP, staff views are audited, and they are embedded in chargeback evidence packets.
- **WebSocket gateway** (`ws/gateway.ts`): first-frame auth (tokens never in URLs), history replay by `afterSeq` on (re)subscribe, per-socket rate limiting, Redis pattern pub/sub for cross-node fan-out.

## 4. Concurrency

- Unique/limited stock uses **optimistic locking**: candidate inventory rows are read without locks and claimed with `UPDATE … WHERE id = $1 AND version = $2 AND status = 'available'`. Manual-stock offers use the offer's `version` column the same way. Seller edits must send the version they read (409 on conflict). Tested with 25 concurrent buyers for one item.
- **Read replica** (`db.ts` `ReadPool`): with `DATABASE_READ_URL`, market search, listings, seller profiles and metrics read from a streaming replica. Anything that decides a write (stock, balances, order state) always reads the primary. If the replica lags more than `REPLICA_MAX_LAG_SECONDS` or stops answering, those reads go to the primary until it recovers; lag is on `/metrics` and `/readyz`.
- **Flash-sale load test** (`apps/api/scripts/loadtest-flash-sale.ts`, `npm run loadtest -w apps/api`): real HTTP, N buyers racing for M items, then database checks. 2,000 buyers for 200 codes at 200 concurrent requests on one API process and one Postgres: exactly 200 sold, no code delivered twice, books balanced; create-order p95 ≈ 1.1s, payment + delivery p95 ≈ 1.0s. The run found that upserting shared ledger accounts took a row lock held for the whole payment transaction, serializing payments (p95 was 3.5–6s); accounts are now read first and only inserted when missing.
- **Search counters** (migration 044, `domain/counters.ts`): listing stock (`offers.available`) and each seller's completed orders and rating (`users.completed_orders`, `rating_avg`, `review_count`) are stored and kept by triggers: inventory changes apply a per-listing delta per statement (concurrent uploads add up), bundle parts refresh their bundles, order status and review visibility update the seller. Search filters and sorts on the columns in its base query. Counting 10,000 live listings went from 2.3 s with per-row subqueries to 5 ms. Order placement still checks the live `offer_available()` under locks, and a worker job repairs any counter that drifted (and logs it).
- **Load checks in CI** (`load` job): a 300-buyer flash sale (no oversell, no double delivery, books balanced, stock counter matches, p95 budgets) and `scripts/loadtest-search.ts` (5,000 listings; text, typo, game-page, rating, instant, directory and autocomplete searches; p95 under 1.5 s on the shared runner, every request 200). On a 4-core dev box: single searches take 7–70 ms; at 20 concurrent, p95 is about 0.5 s with Postgres, the API and the load generator on the same cores.
- Workers claim jobs with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (webhooks) or re-check state under a row lock (releases), so several workers can run safely.

## 5. Security, fraud and compliance

**PCI DSS 4.0.1**
- Card data is only ever entered on the PSP's hosted page (SAQ-A scope). No PAN reaches our servers; we store only brand, last4, PSP card fingerprint and AVS/CVV results.
- 6.4.3 script inventory: `apps/web/payment-scripts.json` lists every script allowed on payment-initiating pages with a justification. `apps/web/scripts/verify-payment-scripts.mjs` runs on every build and fails it on unlisted or inline scripts, logging SHA-384 digests.
- 11.6.1 change/tamper detection: strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`) as a header in `apps/web/nginx.conf`, with violations reported to `POST /v1/security/csp-report` and stored in `csp_reports`.
- MFA for administrative access and quarterly ASV scans are operational controls outside this codebase.

**Encryption and secrets**
- Inventory codes/credentials, webhook secrets and owner-proof evidence: AES-256-GCM with a versioned keyring (`DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`, rotate by appending a key).
- Passwords are **hashed with scrypt**, not encrypted: the spec asked for AES-256 on passwords, but reversible encryption of passwords is weaker than a memory-hard hash, so this is a deliberate deviation.
- API keys are stored as HMACs; OTP codes are stored as HMACs with attempt limits.

**eKYC** (`domain/users.ts`, `providers/kyc/sumsub.ts`): Tier 1 = verified email + SMS OTP (required to buy). Tier 2 = ID document + liveness via Sumsub (required to sell, use the API, and withdraw). Sumsub requests are HMAC-signed and webhooks verified with `X-Payload-Digest`.

**Sessions** (`domain/sessions.ts`): 15-minute access tokens carrying a session id that is checked on every request, so sign-out is immediate. Refresh tokens are opaque, stored as HMACs, rotate on every use, and are delivered to browsers only as an `httpOnly; SameSite=Strict; Path=/v1/auth` cookie (native clients opt into body delivery with `X-Token-Delivery: body`). Replaying an already-rotated refresh token revokes that device's whole session family and emails the user; a 10-second grace window absorbs two tabs refreshing at once. Users can list devices, sign out one or all others; changing the password or 2FA signs out every other device. Open WebSockets are closed with code 4401 when their session ends.

**Email** (`domain/notifications.ts`): transactional outbox written in the same transaction as the event, dispatched by the worker with retries, rendered as text + escaped HTML, sent through Postmark (`EMAIL_PROVIDER=postmark`) or logged in development. Templates cover payment, delivery, refunds, seller sales and payouts, disputes, chargebacks, verification, 24-hour protection-window reminders (sent once), and security alerts (new-device sign-in, password and 2FA changes, stolen refresh token). Users can turn off marketing-style categories; disputes, account and security emails are always on.

**Password reset** (`domain/users.ts`): single-use links valid 30 minutes, stored as HMACs, at most 3 per hour per account; the request endpoint answers identically whether or not the email exists. A reset burns all other outstanding links and signs out every device; two-factor login stays on.

**Phone verification** (`providers/messaging.ts`): SMS via Twilio; Twilio Lookup line-type intelligence rejects VoIP/virtual numbers; one verified number per live account (unique index), which makes farming seller accounts more expensive.

**Reviews** (`domain/reviews.ts`): only buyers of completed orders, one per order, within 30 days; sellers get one public reply; contact details are rejected. Reviews where buyer and seller share a device, card or wallet are stored hidden (sock-puppet pattern) and don't count. Ratings (average, count, % positive) appear on listings and `GET /v1/sellers/:id`. Staff can hide abusive reviews.

**Reconciliation** (`domain/reconciliation.ts`): runs once a day across all workers (advisory-lock guarded) or on demand from `POST /v1/admin/reconciliation/run`. Hard checks: every ledger transaction balances; each fiat order's escrow holding matches its state; the PSP's reported balance equals captured − refunds − PSP fees − chargebacks − seller payouts (PSP processing fees are booked from the capture webhook against treasury). Any mismatch emails every admin. Warnings: negative seller balances, stuck withdrawals, undelivered auto-delivery orders, dead webhooks, failed emails. Platform withdrawals of treasury funds are part of the PSP balance equation.

**Privacy / GDPR** (`domain/privacy.ts`): `GET /v1/me/export` returns the user's data as JSON. `POST /v1/me/delete` (password + 2FA code) requires no open orders and a zero balance, then anonymises the account: name, email, phone, legal name, wallet, 2FA, API keys, webhooks, listings, unsold stock, owner-proof evidence and review text are removed; all sessions are revoked. Orders, ledger, payouts, chargebacks, chat logs and fraud signals are retained without identity for legal-obligation and legal-claims reasons (the chat hash chain stays verifiable). Retention periods for those still need to be set in the privacy policy and enforced by a purge job.

**Market search** (`domain/search.ts`): weighted Postgres full-text search (title > game > description) plus trigram word-similarity so typos and partial words ("eldn", "valornt") still match; filters for category, game, price range, instant delivery, owner-verified accounts, minimum seller rating, in stock and seller; sorts by relevance, newest, price, rating or best-selling; page-based pagination with totals and per-category facet counts; `GET /v1/games?q=` for autocomplete. Stock and rating are computed per query; move them to write-maintained counters if listings pass ~100k.

**Staff audit log** (`domain/audit.ts`): every staff action (dispute decisions, fraud reviews, owner-proof reviews, suspensions, review moderation, manual chargebacks, reconciliation runs) and every staff view of someone else's order, chat or chargeback PDF is written to `admin_audit_log`, which triggers make append-only. Admins browse it in Operations > Audit log or `GET /v1/admin/audit`.

**Seller API docs & sandbox** (`openapi.ts`, `api-schemas.ts`, `routes/sandbox.ts`): the OpenAPI 3.1 document (merchant endpoints and all webhook payloads) is generated from the same zod schemas the routes validate with, served at `/v1/openapi.json` and browsable at `/v1/docs`. A test checks every documented path exists. Sandbox deployments (`SANDBOX_MODE=true`, which refuses to start unless payments and KYC are mocked) add `/v1/sandbox/*`: mark yourself verified, have a simulated buyer purchase your listing, confirm, dispute, skip to the end of the protection window, trigger a chargeback, and flush webhooks.

**Monitoring** (`observability.ts`, `deploy/monitoring/alerts.yml`): JSON logs (pino) with passwords, tokens, cookies and delivery payloads redacted; request ids accepted from `X-Request-Id` and echoed on every response and in 500 errors; Sentry error tracking when `SENTRY_DSN` is set (no headers, cookies or bodies sent); `/metrics` in Prometheus format (protected by `METRICS_TOKEN`, disabled in production without one) with HTTP latency/errors plus business gauges: escrow held, open disputes and their age, chargebacks near deadline, fraud-review backlog, undelivered paid orders, pending/dead webhooks, failed emails, last reconciliation result and age, and per-job worker heartbeats; `/healthz` (liveness) and `/readyz` (database + Redis). Alert rules cover error rate, latency, reconciliation, stalled jobs, chargeback deadlines, dispute backlog, dead webhooks and failing emails.

**Two-factor login** (`domain/mfa.ts`): TOTP (RFC 6238, verified against the RFC test vectors) with ±1 step drift, replay protection (last accepted step is stored), secrets AES-256-GCM encrypted, and 10 single-use recovery codes stored as HMACs. With 2FA on, the password step only returns a 5-minute challenge token that is useless as a session. **Staff actions** (arbiter/admin routes) require a session established with a second factor (PCI DSS 8.4); on by default in production, controlled by `STAFF_MFA_REQUIRED`.

**Payout accounts** (`domain/payouts.ts`): sellers onboard through Stripe Connect Express's hosted flow (bank and identity details never reach our servers). `account.updated` webhooks keep `payouts_enabled` current; withdrawals require it.

**Operations console** (`apps/web/src/pages/Admin.tsx`, `/admin`): dispute queue with links to the order chat, fraud-flag review (clear/unfreeze or confirm/suspend), Original Owner Proof review with the billing-name vs verified-ID check, chargeback deadlines and evidence PDFs, and platform ledger balances. Arbiters see disputes and owner proofs; admins see everything.

**Fraud** (`domain/fraud.ts`, `services/fraud-gnn`)
- Every login/registration/order records graph edges in `risk_signals` (device fingerprint, IP /24 or /48, card fingerprint, wallet). Private/loopback IPs are ignored.
- On payment capture the API computes a **Syndicate Risk Score** inline: shared infrastructure between buyer and seller, connected buyer clusters funnelling into one seller within 72h (union-find over shared signals), new-seller concentration, buyer velocity, links to suspended/charged-back accounts; combined by noisy-OR.
- If `FRAUD_GNN_URL` is set, the **HGT model** (`services/fraud-gnn`) scores the same heterogeneous graph with learned relation-specific attention, falling back to the heuristic within 400ms. `FRAUD_GNN_MODE` decides what its score does: `shadow` (the default) only records it; `enforce` acts on the max of the two.
- **Shadow evaluation** (`fraud_scores`): every scored order keeps the heuristic score, the model score and model version, and the mode in force. A worker labels outcomes as they become known: *fraud* (fraud chargeback, confirmed flag, or a party suspended for fraud) or *legit* (a flag staff cleared, or completed and past `FRAUD_LABEL_LEGIT_DAYS`, default 120). `GET /v1/admin/fraud/model-report` compares the two per model version on the same orders: AUC, and precision, recall and flag counts at the review threshold, plus the orders only one of them would have flagged. Retrain on production labels with `python -m fraud_gnn.train --dsn …` (validates on the newest accounts by default), deploy it, and switch to `enforce` once the report shows it beats the heuristic.
- ≥ 0.5: that order's escrow is frozen, auto-delivery withheld, both parties flagged. ≥ 0.8: the seller's withdrawals and all open escrows are frozen. Admins clear (unfreeze) or confirm (suspend) flags.

**Chargebacks** (`domain/chargebacks.ts`)
- Reason-code map: Visa 10.4 / MC 4837 → fraud evidence (AVS, CVV, IP country vs billing country, prior undisputed orders on the same device/card); Visa 13.3 / MC 4853 → listing snapshot + hash, chat log, whether the buyer ever disputed on-platform, ToS acceptance; Visa 13.6 → refund ledger proof or cancellation policy.
- The PDF packet is generated and submitted to the PSP automatically; response deadlines are 30 days (Visa) / 45 days (Mastercard).
- **Chargeback Shield**: for protected sellers the reserve absorbs the loss; for *High risk · secondary market* listings the seller's balance is debited first. A won chargeback reverses the exact original allocation.

**Backups & restore drills** (`deploy/postgres`, `deploy/backup`): Postgres ships WAL continuously to object storage with WAL-G (`archive_timeout` 60s, so at most about a minute of data is at risk) and takes a daily base backup, keeping 14. The bucket should be in a separate account with versioning and object lock. `restore-drill.sh` restores the latest backup (or a point in time) into a throwaway Postgres, times it, and runs `scripts/verify-restore.ts`: all migrations present, append-only guards installed, every ledger transaction balanced, escrow matching order states, chat sequences complete and hash chains valid. Backups and passing drills record heartbeats, and alerts fire if either goes stale (26 hours / 8 days). `deploy/backup/docker-compose.backup.yml` runs the primary with archiving plus a streaming replica. Uploaded files in S3 need bucket versioning for the same protection.

**Data retention** (`domain/retention.ts`): a daily job (or `POST /v1/admin/retention/run`) deletes order chats and their images 2 years after the order closes, but never while a dispute or chargeback is open. It also deletes idle pre-sale chats after 180 days, erases delivered codes after a year, and removes old fraud signals, order IPs (after 540 days, past the chargeback window), sessions, email records, webhook logs, one-time codes, reset links and CSP reports. The chat log stays append-only, with one exception: this job may delete a whole room. The room keeps its final hash, so a copy exported earlier (e.g. in a chargeback packet) can still be matched. Orders, the ledger, payouts, chargebacks, invoices and the staff audit log are kept for accounting and legal claims. All periods can be configured, and runs are recorded.

**Key management** (`domain/keyrotation.ts`, `providers/signer.ts`): to rotate the data key, append a new key to `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`. The worker then re-encrypts every encrypted field (codes, webhook secrets, 2FA secrets, owner-proof evidence) and every locally stored chat image in batches, with concurrent-safe conditional writes. `GET /v1/admin/keys` shows what is left under each key and which old keys can now be removed. A test fails if a new encrypted column isn't covered. The escrow terms signer can be an AWS KMS secp256k1 key (`ESCROW_SIGNER=aws_kms`): the API only sends digests to KMS, normalises signatures to low-s and derives the recovery id. Production refuses a private key in the environment unless explicitly allowed.

**Notification centre & Web Push** (`domain/inbox.ts`, `providers/push.ts`): every notification lands in the user's inbox, except ones carrying secrets such as reset links. The text is the email subject plus its first paragraph and an in-app link. The inbox has unread counts, mark read / read all, and is kept 180 days. The worker sends new items live to open tabs over the WebSocket and to subscribed devices via standard Web Push (VAPID; Chrome/Android, Firefox, Edge, Safari/iOS web apps). Push preferences are per category, separate from email. Expired subscriptions are dropped. Only known push-service hosts are accepted, and pushes go out through the SSRF guard. Webhooks `payout.paid`, `payout.failed` and `invoice.issued` let merchants automate their books.

**Staff roles** (`domain/permissions.ts`): `arbiter`, `support`, `finance`, `compliance` and `admin`, and a person can hold several. Every staff route asks for a permission, not a role: support can look but not move money, finance moves money but can't suspend people, and compliance investigates but can't pay out. Permissions are read from the database on each request, so changes apply at once. Admins manage access: changes are audited and emailed, nobody can change their own roles, the last admin is protected, and 2FA is required before granting staff access when staff MFA is on.

**AML** (`domain/aml.ts`):
- **Sanctions screening.** Sellers' verified legal names and linked wallets are screened against the OFAC SDN list (names, aliases and published crypto addresses) and any other list supplied in a simple CSV (EU/UK/UN via your data vendor). Matching ignores word order, accents and punctuation (trigram similarity). Screening happens at identity verification, wallet linking, before every payout (if the lists changed or the last check is older than `SANCTIONS_RESCREEN_DAYS`), and in bulk whenever a list changes. Crypto deposits from sanctioned wallets are refused.
- **Monitoring rules (daily).** Rapid pass-through on new accounts, structuring just under a threshold, volume spikes, one buyer funding one seller, and high volume on brand-new accounts.
- **Cases.** Hits open cases for compliance, who are notified. They can clear (a strong match needs a written reason), escalate, pause payouts, or record a suspicious activity report with its FIU reference. Strong matches pause payouts automatically. The user only ever sees a generic "routine review" message, and accounts under review can't be deleted. AML records are kept for the legal period, not by the retention job.

**FX top-ups** (`domain/fxtopups.ts`, `providers/fxexchange.ts`): once a day the planner compares what sellers are owed in each non-settlement currency with what the platform holds there, and proposes buying the shortfall plus `FX_TOPUP_BUFFER_BPS` (default 5%) from the settlement currency, rounded up to a whole unit. Finance and admins are notified. Someone with `fx.manage` approves, and a proposal a person made needs a different person; proposals costing up to `FX_TOPUP_AUTO_APPROVE_CENTS` run without approval (off by default). The exchange (`FX_EXCHANGE_PROVIDER=wise`: a quote for the exact amount, then a balance conversion with an idempotency key; or the mock in development) executes it, and the amounts it actually spent and received are booked as a conversion, so the position and the PSP balance checks follow. A failure is recorded with the reason and the next plan tries again. If the money moved but booking failed, the top-up stays "executing" with the provider's reference for finance to resolve. There is one open proposal per currency; a bigger shortfall replaces an undecided smaller one. With `none`, the planner still proposes and finance records conversions made at the bank by hand.

**Public status page** (`domain/status.ts`, `GET /v1/status`, web `/status`): seven customer-facing components (website, seller API, checkout, delivery and release, chat, payouts, notifications). Each one's status is the worse of what the system measures itself (database and Redis reachable; the worker jobs behind it have a heartbeat under 5 minutes old, else degraded, over 15 minutes an outage; five or more failed payouts in an hour) and any open incident staff posted for it. Staff with `status.manage` (support, admin) post incidents (investigating → identified → monitoring → resolved, with impact) and planned maintenance (scheduled → in progress → completed) with timestamped updates; every action is audited. Visitors subscribe by email with double opt-in, and the worker emails confirmed subscribers on each update, with an unsubscribe link signed per subscriber. The summary is cached for 15 seconds.

**Seller debt collection** (`domain/debts.ts`): a refund after release or a chargeback can leave a seller's balance below zero. Each negative (seller, currency) balance becomes a debt case, checked every `DEBT_CHECK_MINUTES` by one worker. The seller gets reminders on the `DEBT_REMINDER_DAYS` schedule (default at once, day 3 and day 7; only the latest due reminder is sent, never a backlog). After `DEBT_PAUSE_AFTER_DAYS` (default 14) the shop is paused and finance and admins are notified. While anything is owed, withdrawals in every currency are refused (`debt_outstanding`) and the seller can't lift a debt pause from holiday mode. Sellers can repay by card (`POST /v1/seller/debts/:id/pay`, the PSP's hosted checkout; booked `gateway_clearing → seller_available`, so the PSP balance check still holds). New sales pay the debt down automatically. Finance can write a debt off with a four-eyes ledger adjustment. When the balance is back at zero the case closes (`repaid` or `written_off`), and a pause set by debt collection is lifted; a holiday pause the seller set themselves is left alone.

**Resilience** (`docs/RUNBOOK.md`, `scripts/failover-drill.ts`): a drill runs live traffic while taking down Redis, the read replica and every database connection, and while the PSP fails payouts. The latest run: no crashes, 6 failed requests in about 19,700 (all at the moment every database connection was killed), and integrity and reconciliation clean. The drill led to these fixes:
- Redis publishes fail fast instead of hanging requests.
- Dropped database connections no longer crash the process; transactions are retried once if the connection dies before COMMIT.
- Refused replica connections fall back to the primary.
- Readiness depends on the database only.

**Abuse controls**: with `REDIS_URL`, rate-limit counters are shared by every API node. If Redis is briefly down, requests are let through rather than the site going down. CAPTCHA (Cloudflare Turnstile or hCaptcha) protects sign-up and password reset, and sign-in after `CAPTCHA_AFTER_FAILED_LOGINS` failures from one IP within 15 minutes. It is per IP rather than per account, so it can't reveal which emails are registered. `GET /v1/auth/config` tells clients which widget to show.

**Webhooks** (`domain/webhooks.ts`): transactional outbox, `X-Hook-Signature` (hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body), `Idempotency-Key` stable across retries, exponential backoff (30s → 6h cap, ±20% jitter, 12 attempts, then dead-lettered). Merchants can list failing or dead deliveries, redeliver one with the same event id, replay every dead delivery after an outage, re-enable an endpoint and rotate its secret. All of this works from the dashboard or the API (`webhooks:manage` scope). SSRF guard in production (`lib/netguard.ts`): the address check runs inside the socket's DNS lookup, so the address checked is the one connected to (no DNS-rebinding window); blocked ranges include private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT, multicast/reserved and IPv6 forms that embed IPv4 (mapped, NAT64, 6to4); redirects are never followed.

**Hardening from the internal security review**
- Client IPs come from `X-Forwarded-For` only when the hop is a configured proxy (`TRUST_PROXY`); trusting any caller would let attackers pick their IP and dodge per-IP rate limits and fraud signals. Country headers are read only from the configured edge header (`GEO_COUNTRY_HEADER`).
- Per-account sign-in lockout: `LOGIN_MAX_FAILURES` wrong passwords or 2FA codes pause sign-in for `LOGIN_LOCK_MINUTES`, doubling on each repeat (24h cap), with an email to the owner; a successful sign-in resets the counter and a password reset lifts the lock. This stops guessing spread over many IPs, including brute-forcing the 6-digit code behind a stolen password.
- Caller-supplied `X-Request-Id` values are accepted only as plain tokens (no log forging); LIKE wildcards in search input are escaped; malformed upload filenames no longer cause a 500.
- Reviewed and found sound: every admin route checks role (and 2FA for staff), order/chat/attachment reads check participation, JWTs pin HS256, the refresh cookie is `SameSite=Strict`, all SQL is parameterized.

## Known limitations / production hardening

- **External reviews still needed before launch**: a third-party penetration test of the API and web app, an audit of `MarketEscrow.sol`, a PCI QSA/ASV engagement, and legal review of the ToS, refund policy, promo/referral terms, data-retention periods and the per-publisher legality of account trading. The internal review above does not replace them.

- **Fraud model training data.** The shipped model is bootstrapped on synthetic graphs (`fraud_gnn/synthetic.py`). The synthetic task is easy (validation AUC ≈ 1.0), so that number says nothing about production accuracy. Run it in shadow mode (the default), retrain on labelled production data (`python -m fraud_gnn.train --dsn …`, time-based holdout), and only set `FRAUD_GNN_MODE=enforce` once the model report shows it beats the heuristic. The inline heuristic works without it.
- **Validator randomness.** Dispute panels are drawn from a Chainlink VRF v2.5 answer (`MarketEscrow`): the coordinator is fixed at deployment, the callback only stores the seed, anyone then calls `drawPanel`, and anyone can re-request after an hour without an answer. `scripts/deploy.js` refuses to deploy without a coordinator unless `ALLOW_BLOCKHASH_RANDOMNESS=1` (test networks). The subscription must be funded and the contract added as its consumer. The contract still needs an **external audit**; it has unit tests, not an audit.
- **Escrow signer key** should live in a KMS/HSM, not an environment variable.
- **Chat retention**: one user's messages can now be erased early (per-user keys), except messages written before per-user keys were introduced, which only go when their room is purged.
- **Tax**: seeded rates and the treatment rules are a starting point, not tax advice. Invoices have no credit notes yet, because commission is never reversed after release; add them if that changes. US state sales-tax/marketplace-facilitator rules for buyers are not handled.
- **Currencies**: the ledger holds seller balances per currency, but escrow and commission are still settlement-currency only. EUR/GBP top-ups are planned automatically and executed through Wise; with another bank, plug it in behind `FxExchange` or record conversions by hand. The Wise adapter has only been tested against a stubbed API, not a live account.
- **Translations** (site, seller hub, help articles, emails) were written for this release without a professional review; get native speakers to review them (Arabic especially) before marketing in those languages. Staff screens are English only.
- **Moderation terms** start from a short default list; tune it with real listings, and expect both false positives (which land in review, not rejection) and obfuscations it misses.
- The legality of trading game accounts varies by publisher ToS; category policies are a business decision, not something code can settle.
